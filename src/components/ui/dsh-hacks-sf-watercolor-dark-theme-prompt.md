## The core adaptation: watercolor, recolored for a dark theme

A literal warm-pastel watercolor (terracotta bridge, cream houses, sage hills) will clash hard against a near-black background — it reads as a light illustration awkwardly pasted onto dark, rather than belonging there. Instead, adapt the *watercolor painting technique* (soft bleeding edges, translucent washes, loose brushwork) but recolor everything into your existing palette:

- **Wash tones:** instead of warm terracotta/sage/tan, use washes in deep blues, grays, and off-whites — think a moonlit or ink-wash treatment rather than a sunny daytime palette
- **Highlight/accent color:** your existing accent blue (`#5B6EF5`-ish, or whichever blue is current) becomes the color that watercolor "pops" with — bridge cables, window lights, water reflections picked out in that blue against darker washes
- **Structure/linework:** darker charcoal-blue or near-black washes for shadow areas and structural elements (bridge towers, building silhouettes)
- **Highlights:** off-white/cream picked out sparingly — moonlight on water, fog wisps, a lit window — the same off-white already used for your body text
- **Edge treatment:** washes should dissolve into the near-black background at the edges (soft fade to transparent/black), the same way the reference dissolves into white — just inverted

Think "watercolor nocturne" rather than "watercolor postcard" — same painterly technique, moodier palette that belongs on a dark background.

## Sourcing / producing these

Same three routes as before, just with the recolored style prompt:
1. **AI image generation** — lock a consistent style prompt across the set, e.g.: *"watercolor illustration, San Francisco [landmark], deep blue and charcoal wash tones, moonlit/nocturne palette, electric blue accent highlights, dissolving into black background, loose brushwork, painterly"*
2. **Stock watercolor assets**, color-graded/recolored in an image editor to match the blue/black/off-white palette — this is likely the more realistic path if you want a consistent set without generating each individually, since most stock watercolor packs come in warm palettes and will need a deliberate recolor pass
3. **Commission an illustrator**, briefed specifically on the dark nocturne-watercolor direction, not the warm daytime one

Whichever route, generate/recolor the full set in one pass so brush texture, wash density, and the blue-accent treatment stay consistent across all landmarks — mismatched palettes across sections will be more noticeable on a dark theme than they would be on light.
